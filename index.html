Situation, Task, Action, Result

Draft behavioral interview answers piece by piece. Pick a question, write each STAR part with guided prompts and word targets, watch the full answer assemble live, then save it for practice. No external services, a structured writing aid. Everything stays in your browser.

Pick a question to frame the exercise
S. Situation 0 words (aim 30 to 60)
Set the scene in two or three sentences: the context the listener needs, nothing more.
  • Where were you working, and what was your role?
  • What was the context, and what was at stake?
  • What event or constraint kicked this off?
T. Task 0 words (aim 30 to 60)
Your specific responsibility in that situation, stated as a clear goal.
  • What exactly were you responsible for delivering?
  • What was the goal, the deadline, or the bar for success?
  • Why did it land on you and not someone else?
A. Action 0 words (aim 60 to 120)
The meat of the answer: what you personally did, step by step. Say "I", not "we".
  • What did you do first, second, third?
  • What decisions or trade-offs did you make, and why?
  • Who did you bring in, and how did you get them on board?
R. Result 0 words (aim 30 to 60)
The concrete outcome. Include a number if you possibly can: percent, dollars, time saved, people affected.
  • What changed, and can you quantify it?
  • What did the team or the business gain?
  • What did you learn or do differently afterward?

    Tips
    • Quantify the result. "Cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 8" beats "made deploys faster" every time.
    • Say "I", not "we", for your actions. The interviewer is hiring you, not your old team. Credit others, but own your part.
    • Keep the situation short. The most common failure mode is three minutes of backstory. Two sentences of context, then move.
    • One story per question. Do not chain two examples into one answer; pick the stronger story and go deep.
    • End on the result. Land the number, pause, stop talking. Do not trail off into caveats.
    • Practice out loud. 150 to 300 written words is roughly 60 to 90 seconds spoken; time yourself once.
